Scientific Classification

5 Types of Diamonds

Diamonds are scientifically classified by the type and arrangement of chemical impurities (mainly nitrogen or boron).

IaA

Type IaA

Nitrogen atoms in pairs (A-centres). Most common natural diamonds. Usually near-colourless to light yellow.

IaB

Type IaB

Nitrogen in larger aggregates (B-centres). Can impart yellow to brown tint. ~95% of natural diamonds are Type Ia.

Ib

Type Ib

Isolated single nitrogen atoms. Very rare (~0.1%). Produces intense yellow “canary” colour.

IIa

Type IIa

No measurable nitrogen. Chemically purest. Rare. Often colourless; can show pink/brown from lattice strain.

IIb

Type IIb

Contains boron. Extremely rare. Causes blue colour (e.g. Hope Diamond). Also semi-conducting.

Diamond Anatomy

Understanding the parts of a diamond helps you evaluate cut quality and brilliance.

Side View

Top View

Table

Largest flat top facet

Crown

Upper part above girdle

Girdle

Widest outer edge

Pavilion

Lower part below girdle

Culet

Bottom point / facet

Carat

Weight of the diamond. 1 carat = 0.2 grams. Price rises non-linearly with size. Cut quality often matters more than pure size.

Cut

How well the diamond is proportioned and faceted. Excellent / Ideal cut maximises brilliance, fire and scintillation.

Colour

Graded D (completely colourless) to Z. Fancy colours are graded by hue and intensity separately.

Clarity

FL / IF / VVS / VS / SI / I. Most inclusions are microscopic. Eye-clean (VS2–SI1) often offers best value.
